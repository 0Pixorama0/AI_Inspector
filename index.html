In plain English

What this platform actually does

A used laptop goes in. A trusted grade, a photo record and a price signal come out.

Refurbishment centres take in thousands of second-hand devices. Someone has to decide what condition each one is in — is it a clean resale, does it need a repair, or is it only worth parts? That decision sets the price.

Today that job is done by eye. An operator turns the laptop over, notes the scratches on a form, guesses a grade, and moves on. Two people looking at the same device often disagree. When a buyer complains, there is no evidence to point at — just a line in a spreadsheet.

This platform replaces the eye with a camera booth and a trained model. The operator scans the serial, the device is photographed from six angles, and the model marks every defect it finds — scratches, dents, cracked screens, missing keys, swollen batteries — with a confidence score and a severity. The operator confirms or corrects what they see, and the system produces a grade, a health score, and a printable report with the photos attached.

The result is that grading stops depending on who is working that shift. It becomes consistent, fast, and — because every click is logged — something you can defend to a buyer, an auditor, or an insurer.

How it works today
  • Grading is a judgement call — the same laptop gets a B from one operator and a C from another.
  • Condition notes live in a spreadsheet with no photographs attached to them.
  • A buyer dispute takes hours to investigate and usually ends in a credit note.
  • Nobody can tell you which supplier or model is sending in the worst stock.
  • Training a new operator to grade reliably takes weeks.
How it works on the platform
  • One model applies one rule set to every device, on every shift, in every facility.
  • Six photographs and a marked-up defect map are attached to every record, permanently.
  • A dispute is answered by sending a link to the inspection — photos, defects, grade, and who signed it off.
  • Defect trends by model, supplier and intake batch are visible from day one.
  • A new operator follows a guided six-step flow and is productive on their first day.

The use case

One device, end to end

This is the journey the whole product is built around. Everything else in the feature list exists to make one of these seven steps faster, more accurate, or provable after the fact.

01

Intake & identification

A pallet arrives. The operator scans the barcode or lets the camera read the service tag. The platform pulls the model, original spec and any prior inspection history for that serial, so a device that has been round the loop before is flagged immediately.

Operator · 5 seconds
02

Guided positioning

The device is placed in the booth. On-screen guides show the operator exactly where it goes, lid open or closed, for the model in question. The station checks lighting and focus before it will let the capture start.

Operator · 10 seconds
03

Six-angle capture

One trigger fires the whole array — top, bottom, left, right, front and keyboard. Blurred or over-exposed frames are rejected on the spot and re-shot, so a bad photo never becomes a bad grade.

Automated · 8 seconds
04

AI analysis

Images go to the inference service. The model segments each defect, classifies it against the taxonomy, scores its confidence and assigns a severity. A heat map and a set of bounding regions come back attached to the record.

Automated · 12 seconds
05

Operator review

The operator sees the marked-up photographs. They accept what is right, reject false positives, merge duplicates, resize a region that is slightly off, or draw a new one for something the model missed. Every one of those actions is written to the audit trail with a before and after value.

Operator · 20 seconds
06

Grade & disposition

The grading engine turns the confirmed defect set into a cosmetic grade, a functional grade and a health score, using the rule profile for that buyer. It recommends resell, repair, parts or recycle. A supervisor can override, but only with a reason attached.

Automated + Supervisor · 5 seconds
07

Report, hand-off & feedback

A branded PDF certificate is generated and the record is pushed to the ERP by webhook. The buyer gets a read-only link. Meanwhile every operator correction is added to the training set, so next month's model is measurably better than this month's.

Who uses it

Four people, four different products

The same data, surfaced very differently depending on who is looking. Permissions are enforced in the API, not just hidden in the interface.

Role 01

Operator

Lives in the station screen. Scans, captures, reviews detections. Needs speed, big hit targets and a flow that works with gloves on and a queue behind them.

Role 02

Supervisor

Watches the floor in real time, picks up low-confidence and disputed inspections, approves grade overrides, and answers buyer queries with evidence.

Role 03

Administrator

Configures clients, stations, users, defect taxonomy and grading profiles. Owns the integrations, retention policy and the audit export.

Role 04

Client / buyer

Read-only. Sees their own consignments, the grades and the photographic evidence. Never sees another client's data — isolation is enforced at the database row.

Inspection station

The capture workflow the operator actually spends their day in.

  • Automatic device lookup: model, spec, prior inspections, warranty status
  • Six-camera trigger with per-angle retake, focus and exposure validation
  • Offline-tolerant: captures queue locally and sync when the network returns
04

AI detection engine

The model layer — what it looks for and how sure it is.

  • Instance segmentation and classification across cosmetic, display and hardware defects
  • Taxonomy of 20+ classes: scratch, dent, crack, chip, bent chassis, missing key, dead pixel, backlight bleed, hinge play, port damage, corrosion, thermal failure
  • Per-detection confidence, four-level severity, bounding region and heat map
  • Configurable confidence thresholds with auto-escalation to supervisor review
  • Model versioning — every result is stamped with the exact model that produced it
05

AI review & annotation

Human-in-the-loop correction, without slowing the line down.

  • Annotated viewer with bounding boxes, heat map toggle and zoom-to-defect
  • Accept, reject, reclassify, change severity, merge duplicate detections
  • Cursor tool bubble — resize an AI region or draw a missed one directly on the photo
  • Low-confidence queue with second-opinion routing to a supervisor
  • Every correction written to the audit trail and fed back into the training set
06

Grading & disposition

Turning a confirmed defect set into a commercial decision.

  • Separate cosmetic and functional grades (A–F) from a configurable rule set
  • Weighted health score, 0–100, with the contributing defects shown
  • Per-client grading profiles — one buyer's B is another buyer's C
  • Suggested disposition: resell, repair, harvest for parts, or recycle
  • Supervisor override with mandatory reason code, captured in the record

  • Column chooser, density toggle and per-user saved views

Records & reporting

Build detail

The front end

One codebase serves three very different surfaces: a touch kiosk on the floor, a dense back-office application, and a read-only portal for buyers.

The station screen is dark by default — it sits under industrial lighting and is looked at for eight hours straight. Hit targets are sized for gloved hands, the whole capture flow is reachable from the keyboard or a foot pedal, and the interface stays responsive while six full-resolution images upload in the background.

The back office is the opposite problem: a hundred thousand records, sixteen columns, and a supervisor who needs to find one serial number. That is a virtualised table, server-side filtering and saved views — not an infinite scroll.

Design system first

Week one produces a token set — colour, type scale, 8px spacing, radius, motion timing — and the component library built on it. Every screen after that is assembled from those parts, which is what keeps eight weeks of build looking like one product rather than four.

The core API is deliberately boring. All of the interesting engineering sits in the inference service and the grading engine — everything else should be predictable.

Images are the heavy part of this system: six full-resolution photographs per device, thousands of devices a day. They are uploaded straight to object storage with presigned URLs, so the API never becomes the bottleneck, and processing runs as queued jobs that can be retried, re-run against a newer model, or replayed after an outage.

Grading is a rule engine, not hard-coded logic. Defect weights, thresholds and per-client profiles live in the database and are versioned, which means a buyer can change their standard without a release — and any historic record can be re-evaluated against the profile that was live at the time.

Deployment shape

Control plane in the cloud, one edge node per facility. The edge node owns the cameras, the GPU and a local cache; it syncs records upward and pulls model updates downward. Adding a second site is a Terraform apply, not a project.

The model

How the detection engine is built and kept honest

We start from a pretrained segmentation backbone and fine-tune it on your own intake photography. The operator corrections captured in module 05 are the training signal — the system gets better because it is used, not because we rebuild it.

01

Capture

Six calibrated angles, fixed lighting, validated focus and exposure.

02

Pre-process

Device isolation, perspective correction, normalisation, tiling for small defects.

03

Inference

RT-DETR / YOLO-seg fine-tune, exported to ONNX, served on TensorRT.

04

Post-process

NMS, duplicate merging across angles, severity mapping, confidence gating.

05

Human review

Operator accepts, corrects, resizes or draws. Ground truth is created for free.

06

Retrain loop

Corrections queue into the training set; a candidate model must beat the incumbent on a frozen holdout before promotion.

What we need from you
  • 1,500–3,000 representative intake photographs across your actual device mix
  • A defect taxonomy sign-off session in week one — your names, your severities
  • Two operators available for one labelling day per sprint
  • Final camera rig and lighting spec by week three so training data matches production
How accuracy is proven
  • A frozen holdout set is labelled once and never trained on
  • Acceptance gate: recall ≥ 0.90 and precision ≥ 0.85 on the top ten defect classes
  • Per-class scorecard published every sprint — no single blended number
  • Anything below threshold routes to human review rather than being auto-graded

Honest assessment

Risks and how we hold them

RiskLevelMitigation
Not enough labelled training data High Start from a pretrained backbone; run a labelling sprint from week one; use active learning so operator corrections in weeks 5–10 close the gap. Anything under the confidence threshold routes to a human rather than guessing.
Camera rig or GPU hardware arrives late High A mock camera driver and a recorded image set are built in sprint 2, so software delivery is never blocked by hardware. Hardware spec is frozen at week three.
Lighting or positioning varies between sites Medium Fixed rig geometry, a per-station calibration profile, and automatic exposure and focus validation that refuses a bad frame before it reaches the model.
ERP integration access is slow to arrange Medium Contract-first: the connector is built against an agreed schema with a stub, and swapped to the live endpoint when credentials arrive. Not on the critical path.
Grading rules change during the build Medium Grading is configuration, not code. Rules live in versioned profiles that can be edited without a release and replayed against historic records.
Scope grows inside a fixed twelve weeks Medium The sixty features above are the contract. New requests are logged, estimated and scheduled after go-live, or swapped in against something of equal size.
Operator adoption on the floor Low Two of your operators are involved from week one, sit in every demo, and run UAT themselves. The people who will use it help shape it.
Assumptions
  • You provide sample devices covering your real intake mix, plus 1,500–3,000 photographs for training
  • Camera rig, lighting and GPU hardware are procured by you and on site by week three
  • Two operators and one supervisor are available for one day per sprint
  • One integration endpoint (ERP or WMS) is in scope; further systems are change requests
  • Sponsor attends a weekly one-hour review and turns decisions around within two working days
  • Cloud hosting and third-party licences are billed to you at cost
Out of scope for v1
  • Native mobile applications — the web app is responsive and installable
  • Automated repair-cost estimation and parts pricing
  • Marketplace listing sync (eBay, Back Market and similar)
  • Languages other than English in the interface
  • Robotic device handling and conveyor control
  • Functional testing of the device itself — battery cycles, port continuity, screen response
